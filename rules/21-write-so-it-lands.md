# Write so it lands

*Trigger: anything going out with your name on it.*

Anything going out must be understandable with no context, and must not read as machine written.

**Why this exists.** Correct and short is not enough. The reader is missing what sits in your head, and you cannot see that by rereading your own draft. Readers also clock machine-writing habits within a line or two and discount everything after.

**How to apply it.**

- Name the reader first, then set up: every moving piece in plain words before any point, and every term explained the first time it appears. If who reads it is not obvious, I ask.
- Test it on a fresh reader, not yourself. Paste the draft into a new chat and ask it to retell the message, say what the reader should do, and list terms it could not work out. A wrong retelling means rewrite.
- Cut words, never ideas. If your rewrite of my draft comes out longer, I cut too far.
- When a line reads badly, look at structure before vocabulary. Usually an undefined noun, a verb phrase used as the subject, or a comparison missing its other half.
- Strip the tells: no em dashes anywhere, no rule of three (three parallel items where two would do), no "it is not just X, it is Y", no throat clearing (a sentence announcing the point instead of making it), no rest assured or feel free, none of leverage, robust, seamless, delve. Contractions on.
