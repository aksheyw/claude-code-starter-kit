# Pressure-test the expensive decisions

*Trigger: a decision that is expensive to get wrong.*

Put the same question to three reviewers separately, then look at where they disagree.

**Why this exists.** Reviewers who see each other's answers converge on whichever they saw first, so you pay for three opinions and get one. Asked separately they disagree, and the disagreement is the only new information.

**How to apply it.**

- Reviewers here are helpers: separate Claude sessions, each given one job, reporting back (rule 33). Not colleagues. Ask me for them in one message, naming the three jobs.
- Write one neutral brief: what we are deciding, what constrains us, what counts as success. Take your own conclusion out and state it as an open question.
- Three jobs that genuinely differ, not three job titles: one attacks the premise, one speaks only for the customer, one owns the biggest risk.
- Each gets that brief and nothing else. Because a helper starts blank, none can see another's answer, which is the point.
- Each opens with its verdict, then what it checked, what it did not, and what would change its answer. No confidence percentages, per rule 07.
- Then read the three side by side. Surface the disagreement, never average it. Two confident reviewers pointing opposite ways means stop and resolve that fork. Unanimity means your brief led them.
- The panel advises. You decide.
